Many useful LR grammars are intentionally conflicting. A single expression rule is often clearer than a hierarchy of precedence nonterminals, and the dangling-else grammar is naturally stated with a shift policy. In such grammars, conflict resolution selects the parse tree and is therefore part of the language semantics. Practical generators nevertheless merge states before resolving conflicts because canonical LR(1) tables can be large. A merge can create conflicts, or move a real conflict and its resolution into a context where the canonical parser had an unconditional action. This paper asks how to compact an LR table without changing where its semantic choices occur or how they are decided. The key representation encodes "reduce r on a" as an ordinary transition labeled a into a state distinguished by production r, so shifts, gotos, and reductions inhabit one labeled transition system. Subset construction removes nondeterminism in where the machine goes; incompatible parser actions remain as output nondeterminism. Gazelle resolves or explicitly defers that choice in the canonical machine, before context is discarded. A conservative completion aligns reductions across same-core states, and ordinary partition refinement computes the quotient while preserving accepted-input action traces. On five tractable grammars Gazelle's resolved canonical machine is checked action by action against GNU Bison's canonical-LR output by a bisimulation; after completion and minimization its state counts agree with Bison's IELR mode on six grammars. A normalized SQLite grammar falls from 66,539 canonical states to 1,340, matching IELR, in about six seconds; Bison's canonical mode did not finish within seven minutes. The result is a direct route to a compact, canonical-LR-equivalent parser that preserves intentional conflict semantics.
